What if one of the least useful keys on your keyboard could become one of the most useful?

## Reclaiming Caps Lock

Caps Lock occupies some of the easiest pinky keyboard real estate, but most people rarely use it. Meanwhile, genuinely useful keys are stuck at the edges of the keyboard. Escape sits all the way in the upper-left corner, and backslash, which is used constantly in LaTeX, is on the opposite side. Keyboard shortcuts can be even worse, requiring awkward combinations of several keys.

[Karabiner-Elements](https://karabiner-elements.pqrs.org/) offers a solution. It is a **free keyboard customization tool for Mac**. macOS can natively swap the behavior of modifier keys, but the available options are limited. Karabiner gives you much more control, allowing a key or key combination to produce an entirely different input.

![Karabiner-Elements app icon](assets/karabiner_logo.jpeg)

## Turn Caps Lock Into Whatever You Need

To give you an idea of what is possible, here is how I use it: I map Caps Lock to `F18`, a key that does not exist on my physical keyboard but can still be used as a virtual key. I then configured my voice-to-text tool, [Handy](/blog/2026/09/handy-open-source-speech-to-text/), to start listening when I press F18. The result is that I can press and hold `Caps Lock` to type with my voice!

That is only one possibility. You could map Caps Lock to Escape, use it to make an awkward shortcut easier, or turn it into a button for another tool you use every day.

If Caps Lock is going unused on your keyboard, [check out Karabiner-Elements](https://karabiner-elements.pqrs.org/) and give that valuable key a better job. If you find a useful way to remap it, please let me know what you turned it into!
