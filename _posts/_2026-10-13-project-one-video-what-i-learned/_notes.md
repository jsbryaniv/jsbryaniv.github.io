- 9 scenes, 1920x1080, 30fps

## The bet: a code-based editor
- Remotion — video assembled in React/TypeScript, not a GUI timeline
- One file per script section in `code/src/scenes/`
- `theme.ts` — colors, circle size/position, phone + window frames, face framing
- `timing.ts` — lead-in, tail, cross-fade length
- `narration.json` — per-scene durations, generated by `pipeline/write_timeline.py`
- Point worth making: changing one number in `timing.ts` restyled every transition
  in the video at once. You can't do that in a timeline.
- Point worth making: the video is a git-able text project, so an AI agent could
  edit it. That's the real unlock, and it's the same thesis as the video's own
  three-files-in-Notion idea — keep the source of truth in plain text you own.

## Pipeline, five stages
1. `pipeline/transcribe.sh` — ffmpeg audio extract + whisper.cpp (large-v3-turbo, 1.6 GB local)
2. `pipeline/match_takes.py` — picks final takes, writes a human-readable review report
3. `pipeline/cut_clips.py` — ffmpeg cuts the approved plan into `clips/narration/`
4. `pipeline/remove_background.py` — RobustVideoMatting → transparent WebM in `clips/keyed/`
5. `pipeline/sync_screen.py` — cuts screen recordings out of one OBS take
- Deliberate constraint: Python standard library + ffmpeg only, by choice. No deps.

## What went wrong
- **Chromakey failed.** Cheap, unevenly lit, wrinkled green screen. ffmpeg
  `chromakey` ate the navy shirt and his hair; `hsvkey` left shadow halos.
  Test images in `output/key-tests/`. Rejected 2026-10-02.
  Fix: RobustVideoMatting — AI person segmentation, not color-based.
- **Keying is slow.** ~1.7–3 frames/second on the M1. 95 minutes for nine clips
  at full 1080x1920. Note: half resolution would have been plenty for how the
  footage is actually used — circle in a corner.
- **Matching the script to the transcript was too brittle.** Original plan was
  fuzzy-match script paragraphs against the transcript with `difflib`. But he
  rewords heavily while recording, so many spoken lines matched nothing in
  `script.md`. Redesign: split the *transcript* into spoken phrases, detect a
  retake when a phrase repeats something already said, drop everything from the
  earlier version onward. Script now only decides which section a phrase is in.
- **Retakes weren't paragraph-shaped.** They happen at sentence level too. And
  in a few spots he read a whole section, then redid *only the final paragraph* —
  so the right clip is a stitch of two non-adjacent moments, not one cut.
- **Whisper's default word timings were unusable.** Needed `-dtw` with `-nfa`.
- **Pauses.** Anything over 2.5s inside a kept take gets trimmed (`MAX_PAUSE`).
- **One 12-minute raw clip held all nine sections'** narration, not just the intro.

## What worked better than expected
- **Recording the screen captures LAST, against the rendered video.** Scenes
  rendered with placeholders that displayed: the exact filename to save as, the
  script's directions, and a running clock to record against. Drop
  `clips/screenshare/<name>.mp4` in and it swaps the placeholder automatically.
- **Then: one OBS take, cut automatically.** Played the video in Remotion Studio
  while screen-recording with computer audio, so the take contains the narration.
  `sync_screen.py` finds where each section's narration plays and cuts that
  stretch out (last complete run wins). Five of six recordings came from one take
  (`clips/raw/2026-10-06 15-04-09.mp4`).
- **Human-in-the-loop by design.** Nothing is cut until he's read the review
  report. `match_takes.py` flags stitched clips and low-confidence matches.
  There was also an audio-only preview render to listen to the cut plan before
  any video was cut (`output/Video1 Intro.cut-preview.m4a`).
