- Intro (look at previous posts for exmaples)
- Last week I promised to have progress on the 3d printing products, and I actually do! Though I still don't have anything printed...
- So clearly over the last few weeks, my initial hypothesis for using 3d printing as a low cost means to establish product-market fit was severly challenged by the logistics of printing my designs on a printer I do not own. Extrapolating long term, I was becoming somewhat worried that if these issues persisted when it came to start selling products, then it would be really bad. I wouldn't want to sell someone something and have them wait a few weeks for me to print it.
- But behold I found a solution! This solution is inspired by my recent success with Amazon print on demand. And this solution is completely obvious in hind sight.
- Enter: on-demand 3D print drop shipping. Basically, I found several companies that will do on-demand 3D printing and handle the shipping logistics. All I need to do is design the product, upload my 3D model, then link my store. Then whenever someone purchases a product from my store these 3rd party vendors will print it and ship it to them. The only upsetting part is that I wish I knew about this earlier!
- There are many vendors that do this, the one I am looking at first is called Printie. I took the first step by uploading a design and ordering a prototype to be shipped to my house. It should be here next week. If it looks good then I can finish the process and add it to the shep4 store!
